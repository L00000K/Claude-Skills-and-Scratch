# Conceptual Site Model — Sizewell C Nuclear Power Station

**Site:** Sizewell C (SZC)
**Location:** Near Leiston, Suffolk, England
**Grid Reference:** TM 472 224 (approximate site centre)
**Developer:** NNB Generation Company (SZC) Ltd (EDF Energy / NNB GenCo)
**Proposed Technology:** Two EPR (European Pressurised Reactor) units
**Document Status:** Indicative conceptual model based on publicly available information
**Date:** April 2026

---

## 1. Introduction

This Conceptual Site Model (CSM) describes the environmental setting of the proposed Sizewell C (SZC) nuclear power station site on the Suffolk coast of eastern England. It synthesises geological, hydrogeological, ecological, and land-use information to define the source–pathway–receptor (SPR) framework that underpins environmental risk assessment, hydrogeological impact assessment, and regulatory submissions under the Planning Act 2008 (Development Consent Order).

The CSM draws on the SZC Environmental Statement (ES) submitted to the Planning Inspectorate, published technical reports, British Geological Survey (BGS) mapping, Environment Agency data, and Natural England designations.

---

## 2. Site Description

### 2.1 Location and Setting

Sizewell C is located on the Suffolk Heritage Coast, approximately 4 km east of Leiston and 25 km south of Lowestoft. The main construction site boundary covers approximately 73 ha of low-lying coastal plain immediately north of the existing Sizewell B Pressurised Water Reactor (PWR) and adjacent to the partially decommissioned Sizewell A Magnox station. The North Sea lies approximately 50 m to the east of the proposed reactor platform.

**Key distances to sensitive features:**
- Leiston town centre: ~3 km west
- RSPB Minsmere Nature Reserve (northern boundary): ~0.5 km north
- Sizewell B (operational PWR): immediately south
- Sizewell A (decommissioning): immediately south of Sizewell B

### 2.2 Topography

The site occupies a level to gently undulating coastal plain at 2–10 m above Ordnance Datum (AOD). The ground is underlain by Quaternary deposits that thin toward the coast. The beach ridge and foreshore are at or near sea level. A series of low ridges and hollows reflects the underlying glacial and periglacial morphology.

### 2.3 Historical and Current Land Use

| Period | Land Use |
|--------|----------|
| Pre-1950s | Agricultural (arable and pasture), coastal heath |
| 1960s–1970s | Construction of Sizewell A (Magnox, operational 1966) |
| 1980s–1990s | Construction and operation of Sizewell B (PWR, operational 1995) |
| 2000s–present | Sizewell A decommissioning; Sizewell B operation; remaining land as agricultural/heath |

### 2.4 Project Scope

The SZC project includes:
- Two EPR reactor units (~3.2 GW combined)
- Cooling water intake and outfall structures in the North Sea
- Transmission infrastructure (National Grid connection)
- Temporary construction village (~2,400 workers)
- Upgraded road and rail connections (A12 junction, potential rail spur)

## 3. Geological Setting

### 3.1 Regional Geology

The site lies within the Suffolk Coastal Plain, a low-lying terrace underlain by a sequence of Quaternary glacial and interglacial sediments resting on Neogene–Palaeogene bedrock. The regional geological succession from surface to depth is:

| Formation | Age | Lithology | Approx. Thickness at Site |
|-----------|-----|-----------|---------------------------|
| Made Ground / Beach Deposits | Recent–Holocene | Sand, gravel, shell debris | 0–2 m |
| Blown Sand / Alluvium | Holocene | Fine sand, peat lenses | 0–3 m |
| Glacial Till (Lowestoft Till) | Late Pleistocene | Chalky sandy boulder clay | 5–20 m |
| Glaciofluvial Sands and Gravels | Pleistocene | Medium–coarse sands, flint gravel | 0–15 m (variable) |
| Norwich Crag | Early Pleistocene | Marine shelly sands and silts | 20–40 m |
| Red Crag | Pliocene–Early Pleistocene | Ferruginous shelly sands | 0–10 m (present locally) |
| London Clay | Eocene (Palaeogene) | Dark grey silty clay, pyrite | 50–100 m |
| Thanet Sand Formation | Palaeocene | Fine-grained glauconitic sand | 10–20 m |
| Chalk (Upper Chalk) | Late Cretaceous | White chalk, flint bands | >200 m |
| Gault Clay | Cretaceous | Dark silty clay | 30–50 m (at depth) |

### 3.2 Structural Geology

The site lies within a structurally simple platform area. No active faults are mapped at surface within the immediate site vicinity. The Mesozoic and Palaeogene strata dip gently eastward at 1–2°. The eastern margin of the London Platform and the North Sea Basin transition occurs offshore.

**Geohazards:**
- **Seismicity:** The eastern UK has low seismic hazard; the closest historically significant seismic event was the 1884 Colchester earthquake (ML ~4.6, ~60 km SW). Peak Ground Acceleration (PGA) at 10,000-year return period is low. SZC EPR design includes seismic qualification per UK nuclear safety standards.
- **Ground settlement:** Soft Holocene alluvium and peat in low-lying areas present a compressibility risk relevant to infrastructure design.
- **Coastal erosion:** Active erosion of the beach ridge (~0.2 m/year historically); coastal protection and sea-level rise adaptation are key design considerations.

## 4. Hydrogeological Setting

### 4.1 Superficial Aquifer (Unconfined)

The glaciofluvial sands and gravels of Pleistocene age form a discontinuous unconfined aquifer within the superficial deposits. Water table depth is typically 1–3 m below ground level (bgl) and exhibits strong seasonal variation (±1–2 m). Groundwater flow is broadly eastward toward the North Sea, influenced by tidal loading within ~200 m of the coast. Hydraulic conductivity of the sands and gravels: 1×10⁻⁴ to 1×10⁻³ m/s (estimated from BGS borehole logs and regional data).

### 4.2 Norwich Crag Aquifer (Semi-confined)

The Norwich Crag marine sands form a regionally extensive Secondary A aquifer beneath the till confining layer. This aquifer supports limited private abstractions in the surrounding area. Groundwater is typically under artesian or sub-artesian pressure beneath the till. Recharge occurs principally through windows in the till and by lateral flow from inland outcrop areas.

### 4.3 Chalk Aquifer (Principal Aquifer)

The Upper Chalk is a Principal Aquifer of major regional importance, providing public water supply across East Anglia. At the SZC site, the Chalk is overlain by the London Clay (50–100 m thick), which acts as a thick aquitard. Chalk groundwater at the site is therefore deeply confined and not directly influenced by near-surface activities. Regional Chalk groundwater flow is broadly northeastward toward the North Sea.

**Abstraction:** The nearest major public water supply abstraction from the Chalk is at Aldeburgh (~8 km south). Private Chalk abstractions exist in the wider area.

### 4.4 London Clay as Aquitard

The London Clay (Eocene) forms a low-permeability confining layer (hydraulic conductivity ~1×10⁻¹¹ to 1×10⁻¹⁰ m/s) that effectively isolates the Chalk aquifer from overlying superficial and Crag groundwaters. This is a key feature of the CSM that limits downward migration of any near-surface contamination.

### 4.5 Surface Water

| Feature | Description | Approximate Distance from Site |
|---------|-------------|-------------------------------|
| North Sea | Tidal saline; cooling water source/receptor | 0–50 m (east) |
| Sizewell Belts drainage ditches | Network of reed bed drainage channels; flow to Minsmere | within site boundary |
| River Minsmere | Meandering lowland river; flows east to Minsmere Haven | ~500 m north |
| Minsmere Haven / Sluice | Outfall of River Minsmere to sea | ~1 km north |
| Leiston Stream / Abbey Stream | Drains agricultural land west of site | ~1.5 km west |
| Minsmere Levels (RSPB managed) | Lowland wet habitat; controlled water levels | 0.5–2 km north |

**Surface water classification (WFD):** The River Minsmere is currently classified as Moderate ecological status (Environment Agency). The North Sea coastal waters are classified under the Water Framework Directive.

### 4.6 Coastal Hydrogeology

Tidal influence on shallow groundwater is expected within ~200–300 m of the shoreline. Saline intrusion into the superficial aquifer is possible along the coastal fringe. The existing Sizewell B cooling water intake/outfall creates a localised thermal plume in near-shore waters.

## 8. Conceptual Model Summary

The Sizewell C site presents a moderately complex environmental setting characterised by:

1. **Layered aquifer system:** A shallow unconfined superficial aquifer (sands/gravels) is semi-separated from the Norwich Crag aquifer by variable glacial till. The deeply confined Chalk Principal Aquifer is effectively protected by the thick London Clay aquitard (~50–100 m), substantially reducing contamination risk to this strategically important water resource.

2. **Ecologically sensitive immediate context:** The site is flanked to the north by one of England's most important wildlife sites — RSPB Minsmere and the Minsmere–Walberswick SPA/Ramsar/SSSI. Any surface water or groundwater pathway connecting the construction or operational site to these habitats is a critical environmental linkage.

3. **Coastal dynamics:** Sea-level rise, storm surge, and long-term coastal erosion present increasing physical hazard to the site over the plant's 60+ year operational life. Cooling water thermal discharge to the North Sea is the principal operational water-quality linkage, governed by Environmental Permit.

4. **Nuclear legacy adjacency:** Legacy groundwater contamination from Sizewell A is managed under regulatory oversight. The SZC development must be designed to prevent interference with the existing contamination management regime and to avoid creating new unacceptable pollutant linkages.

5. **Construction dominates near-term risk:** During the multi-year construction programme, dewatering, earthworks in London Clay and Quaternary deposits, and hydrocarbon handling represent the most plausible pathways for mobilising contamination toward the ecologically sensitive Sizewell Belts drainage network and Minsmere.
